## 1. Recover lost root-canal visits

**What happened:** I find revenue fell estimated ~$172K CAD (3.9%) and operating profit fell 21% from 2024 to 2025.

**Why:** I find broadly steady average charges; 55% of the revenue decline came from 26% fewer root-canal services, averaging estimated $1,347 CAD/service. I find rent and salaried staff costs unchanged, so costs fell more slowly than revenue.

**What it's worth:** I estimate recovering 36 of the 72 lost services (50%) could add estimated ~$48K CAD/year in service revenue, before extra costs.

**What to do:** I would check referrals sent elsewhere, dentist availability and accepted-but-unbooked treatment plans.

**How to measure:** I would track root-canal services per month and the treatment-plan acceptance rate.

## 2. Confirm long-lead bookings

**What happened:** I find the no-show rate is 9.3% for 30+-day bookings versus 3.0% for bookings under 7 days, about 3.1 times as high.

**Why:** I find the same rising pattern in 3 of 3 branches, so I would test whether reminders and confirmations improve attendance.

**What it's worth:** I estimate matching the 7–29-day rate could retain about 230 visits/year and add estimated ~$57K CAD/year in service revenue, before extra costs.

**What to do:** I would pilot a reminder 7 days before care and a reply-to-confirm 48 hours before care at one branch. I would have staff review unconfirmed slots before offering them to a short-notice waitlist.

**How to measure:** I would track the no-show rate for 30+-day bookings and slots refilled against a comparison branch.

## 3. Collect older unpaid bills

**What happened:** I find estimated ~$447K CAD of estimated ~$633K CAD owed is over 90 days old at 2025-12-31.

**Why:** I would check whether insurer delays, patient balances or billing errors explain the older bills. I note an estimated ~$314K CAD timing lift in 2024 cash because tracked unpaid operating bills start at zero.

**What it's worth:** I estimate recovering 25% of 91–180-day bills could release estimated ~$17K CAD in cash, before collection costs. I would not count this as new service revenue or subtract unpaid bills from profit.

**What to do:** I would run a collections process for 60–180-day bills (estimated ~$89K CAD), classifying each balance as insurer, patient or billing-error work.

**How to measure:** I would track days to collect and the unpaid balance over 90 days.

## 4. Prioritize Maple for the reminder pilot

**What happened:** I find Maple has a 6.3% no-show rate versus 4.6% at Harbour; the difference is statistically significant (two-sided p < 0.001).

**Why:** I also find Maple above Harbour within every booking lead-time band, so I would investigate its confirmation workflow.

**What it's worth:** I estimate matching Harbour could retain about 100 visits/year and add estimated ~$25K CAD/year in service revenue, before extra costs. I would count only the non-overlapping part alongside the group reminder opportunity.

**What to do:** I would prioritize Maple for the reminder and confirmation pilot.

**How to measure:** I would track Maple's no-show rate and its change against Harbour, split by booking lead time.
